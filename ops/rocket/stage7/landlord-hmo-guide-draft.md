## Choosing beds for a rental or HMO: a practical checklist

If you let furnished rooms, the bed is the piece of furniture that works hardest and gets checked most. This checklist covers what to think about before you buy: room size, storage, wear and tear, when to replace, delivering to several properties, and the fire-safety paperwork you should ask any supplier for.

### 1. Match the bed to the room

Measure the room first, then choose the bed. Standard UK mattress sizes are:

| Size | Typical mattress size |
|---|---|
| Single (3ft) | 90 x 190 cm |
| Small Double (4ft) | 120 x 190 cm |
| Double (4ft 6in) | 135 x 190 cm |
| King (5ft) | 150 x 200 cm |
| Super King (6ft) | 180 x 200 cm |

A bed frame is larger than its mattress, especially one with a headboard or wings. Check the exact frame size on each product page before you order. **[BLOCKED: owner fact: confirmed outside dimensions per design and size are not yet on all product pages (Stage 6 blocker); do not add our frame dimensions to the article until confirmed.]**

**HMOs in England:** under the HMO licensing rules that came into force on 1 October 2018, a room used for sleeping by one person aged over 10 must be at least 6.51 square metres. The rules set larger minimums for rooms shared by two people. Floor where the ceiling is under 1.5 m does not count. Your council can set higher standards. Check the current rules for your property on legislation.gov.uk (SI 2018/616) and with your council. Scotland and Wales have their own rules.

Rules of thumb:
- In a single HMO room near the minimum size, a single or small double usually leaves space for a desk and wardrobe.
- In a double room let to a couple, a double (4ft 6in) or king fits most layouts.
- Leave enough space to open doors, drawers and an ottoman lid fully.

### 3. Think about wear and tear

Rented beds are used by more people over their life than a bed at home. When you compare beds, ask:

- **How much weight does the frame hold?** Our beds hold up to 300 kg.
- **What is the warranty?** Our frames have a 5-year warranty and our mattresses a 5-year guarantee. **[BLOCKED: owner fact Q20/Q21: whether the same warranty, returns and damage terms apply to trade orders.]**
- **Can the fabric be cleaned or replaced?** Fabric choice matters more in a let than at home. **[BLOCKED: owner fact Q15: whether there is a hard-wearing or easy-clean fabric for rentals. - **Is the mattress separate from the base?** A separate mattress can be replaced on its own, which is cheaper than replacing the whole bed.
- **Can it be taken apart?** Narrow stairs in older houses make this important. Ask before you order.

### 4. Plan replacements, not emergencies

- Inspect every bed and mattress at each check-out and record its condition with photos.
- Keep a list of each bed's size, design and fabric per room. Ordering a matching replacement is then one message.
- Mattresses usually need replacing before frames. Budget for that separately.
- Made-to-order beds take time to make. Order replacements before a changeover, not on the day.

### 5. Delivery to several properties

If you are furnishing more than one house or flat, put this in your enquiry:

1. Each delivery postcode and how many beds go to each address.
2. Access: floor level, stairs, lifts, parking, and who will let the driver in.
3. Whether the bed should be left downstairs, taken to the room, or assembled.
4. The date the property must be ready (for example a tenancy start).

### 6. Fire safety: what to ask any supplier

If you let furnished property in the course of business, the furniture you provide must meet the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 (as amended). Council guidance says the rules apply to landlords and letting agents, and cover beds, headboards and mattresses as well as sofas and chairs. Your council enforces them.

The labels work differently for different items:

- **Mattresses, divans and bed bases** do not carry the permanent furniture label. Instead they should be labelled as meeting **BS 7177** (the British Standard for resistance to ignition of mattresses, divans and bed bases).
- **Headboards and other upholstered items** generally need a **permanent label** sewn or fixed on, showing they meet the Regulations.
- **Second-hand furniture** must meet the same rules when you supply it in a let.

Questions to ask any bed supplier before you buy:

1. Does each mattress and divan base carry a BS 7177 label?
2. Does each upholstered headboard or bed frame carry the permanent label required by the 1988 Regulations?
3. Can you send the test certificates for the fabric and fillings?
4. If I supply my own fabric, what is needed (for example an interliner or a fabric test certificate) for the finished bed to meet the Regulations?
5. Will the labels still be attached when the bed is delivered, so I can photograph them for my records?

Keep photos of the labels and any certificates with your property records.

### 7. Quick checklist

- [ ] Room measured; bed size chosen to leave space for doors, drawers or ottoman lid
- [ ] Storage type chosen (drawers or ottoman) for each room
- [ ] Weight limit and warranty checked
- [ ] Fabric choice suits a let; swatches seen
- [ ] Mattress planned as a separate, replaceable item
- [ ] Fire labels asked for: BS 7177 on mattress and base, permanent label on upholstered items
- [ ] Delivery addresses, access and ready-by dates listed
- [ ] Room-of-choice drop off or assembly decided per bed
- [ ] Records kept: photos of labels, invoices, room-by-room bed list
