What to check before you buy

  • Frame material. Ask what the frame is made from. Solid timber is strong and can be repaired; some low-cost frames use particle board or thin metal. If the listing does not say, ask the seller. Every Eagle Bed is built on a solid timber frame.
  • Slats. The slatted base supports your mattress and lets it breathe. Look at how many slats there are, how far apart they are, and whether there is a centre support rail on larger sizes. Some mattress guarantees depend on the base, so check your mattress terms too.
  • Gas struts on ottoman beds. A gas-lift ottoman uses struts to lift the mattress platform. Ask whether the struts suit the weight of your mattress, whether the bed opens from the end or the side, and whether replacement struts are available. End-lift needs clear floor at the foot; side-lift suits beds pushed against a wall.
  • Fabric and rub tests. Upholstery fabric is often rated with a Martindale rub test: a higher figure means the fabric resisted more wear in testing. Ask for the figure if hard wear matters to you, for example with pets or children. Order swatches so you can see colour and texture in your own light.
  • Delivery and assembly. Check who delivers, whether it is a courier or the seller's own team, whether the bed is carried to the room, and whether assembly is included or extra. Ask about delivery timescales for made-to-order items.
  • Returns. Read the returns policy before you pay. How many days do you have, who pays for collection, and what condition must the item be in? Made-to-order and mattress returns often have extra conditions.
  • Warranty. Look for a written warranty that says how long the frame is covered and what counts as a fault. Keep your order confirmation.
  • Finance. Buy-now-pay-later and instalment plans are useful, but they are credit. Check the provider's terms, whether a credit check is involved and what happens if you miss a payment.

Buying direct from a maker or from a big retailer

Buying direct from a maker usually means more choice of fabric, colour and size, because the bed is made for your order. You can speak to the people who build it, ask practical questions about headboard heights or awkward stairs, and deal with the maker if anything needs fixing later. Prices can be good value because there is no middleman. The trade-off is time: a made-to-order bed is not sitting in a warehouse, and a small business may not have a showroom near you.

A big retailer may suit you better if you need a bed within a day or two, want to lie on beds in a store before choosing, want a long mattress trial, or are furnishing a whole house and want one order and one delivery. Big retailers also sell wooden and metal beds, which some makers, including us, do not make.

Neither choice is right for everyone. Use the checklist above and compare like with like.
