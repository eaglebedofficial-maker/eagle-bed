Super King vs King: what is the difference?

A king (5ft) is 150 x 200 cm and a super king (6ft) is 180 x 200 cm. Both are 200 cm long, so the difference is width: a super king is 30 cm wider, which gives each sleeper about 90 cm, the width of a single bed. A king gives each sleeper about 75 cm. Choose a super king if you have a bedroom of roughly 3.5m x 3.5m or larger and want to stretch out; choose a king if the room is nearer 3m x 3m, or if you want to leave more floor space. A tall statement headboard suits both. Browse wingback beds and Chesterfield beds, which are made in both sizes. Ready to choose? Shop king size beds or super king beds.

Small Double vs Double: which one do you need?

A small double (4ft) is 120 x 190 cm and a double (4ft6) is 135 x 190 cm. They are the same length; the double is 15 cm wider. A small double is a sensible upgrade from a single in a spare room or small bedroom, and it can fit where a 4ft6 would block a wardrobe door. A double suits two adults in an average bedroom, although two adults will have about 67 cm each, so consider a king if comfort matters more than floor space. Room-size tips: how much space do you need?

As a rule of thumb, leave at least 60 cm of clear floor on each side you will walk down, and more where you open wardrobes or drawers. Adding the mattress width to 60 cm either side gives a minimum wall width:

Size Mattress width Minimum wall width with 60 cm each side
Single 90 cm 210 cm
Small Double 120 cm 240 cm
Double 135 cm 255 cm
King 150 cm 270 cm
Super King 180 cm 300 cm

Allow extra for the headboard and the frame's upholstered sides, and 60 cm or more at the foot of the bed. If the bed sits against a wall on one side, you need less width but must still reach both sides to make it. For a step-by-step method, read how to measure your bedroom for a new bed.

Choosing storage by size

Storage matters more as the bed gets bigger, because a king or super king takes up floor space a chest of drawers would have used. How to measure before you order

  1. Measure the wall the bed will sit against, and the two walls either side.
  2. Allow 60 to 75 cm of clear floor for walking and for opening drawers or wardrobes.
  3. For a drawer storage bed, allow roughly the drawer's own depth of clear floor at the side or foot.
  4. For an ottoman bed, leave clear space at the foot or side for the base to lift.
  5. Measure the wall height above the mattress line if you are considering an extra-tall headboard.
  6. Check the route in: doorways, stair turns and landing width.

What is the size of a small double bed in the UK?

A UK small double bed is 4ft wide and takes a 120 x 190 cm mattress. A double (4ft6) is 135 x 190 cm, so a small double is 15 cm narrower and the same length. Allow a few centimetres extra for an upholstered frame, and check the size selector on each bed's page.

Is the bed frame bigger than the mattress?

Yes. Upholstered frames add a few centimetres either side, and the headboard adds to the overall length, so measure for the frame as well as the mattress.
